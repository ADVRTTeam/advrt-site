Ask ten surveying firms what a lead costs and you'll get ten invoice prices. Ask what an instruction costs and the room goes quiet. That second number is the one your business actually runs on, and most firms have never worked it out.

## The invoice price is the least interesting number

A shared lead from a comparison site looks cheap on paper. But it's sold to you and several competitors at once, and the homeowner has usually filled in one form with no idea who any of you are. You're not buying a client. You're buying a place in a queue.

So the firm that wins that job is rarely the best surveyor. It's the one who called back in four minutes rather than four hours. If you can't consistently be that firm, a good chunk of what you spend on shared leads is a donation to whoever can.

## The three numbers that actually matter

Strip the noise away and your pipeline runs on three figures:

- **Cost per enquiry.** Everything you spend on a channel in a month, divided by the enquiries it produced. Not clicks. Enquiries.
- **Enquiry-to-instruction rate.** Of those enquiries, how many became booked jobs? This is where shared and exclusive leads part company.
- **Average fee.** What an instruction is worth to you, by survey type. A Level 3 on a period property is not a Level 2 on a flat.

Put them together and you get cost per instruction. As an illustration: say a channel gives you enquiries at £30 each and you convert one in five. Your instructions cost £150. A "cheaper" channel at £18 an enquiry where you convert one in twelve is costing you £216 a job, plus the hours your team spent chasing the other eleven. The cheap channel is the expensive one.

> You don't have a lead cost problem. You have a conversion visibility problem.

## Why exclusive beats cheap

An enquiry from your own landing page behaves differently. The homeowner searched for the thing you do, read a page about exactly that service, and chose to contact you by name. There's no race because there's no queue. Nobody else got the lead.

That intent shows up everywhere downstream: they answer the phone, they've usually got the property details ready, and they're comparing you against doing nothing rather than against four rivals. Exclusive enquiries tend to cost more per lead and convert at a rate that makes the comparison-site maths look silly.

## How to find your number

Three moves, in order:

- **Track attribution properly.** Every enquiry should carry its source with it: which campaign, which page, which search. If your tracking can't trace an instruction back to the click that started it, fix that before spending another pound.
- **Make your forms do the qualifying.** A two-stage quotation form that captures property type, value, location and timescale hands you everything you need to price the job, and quietly filters out the work you don't want.
- **Answer fast.** Whatever your channel mix, response speed is the cheapest conversion upgrade there is. Minutes, not hours.
