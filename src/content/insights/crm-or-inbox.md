There is a particular kind of waste we see regularly: a firm doing six enquiries a week buys an enterprise CRM, spends three months configuring it, and quietly goes back to the inbox because nobody could face the admin.

So let us start from the unfashionable position. An inbox is a legitimate lead handling system. At low volume, with one or two people handling enquiries and a reasonable discipline about flags and folders, it is fast, familiar and free. If that is you, the honest advice is to keep it and spend the money on generating more enquiries instead.

The question is not whether an inbox is sophisticated. It is whether you have outgrown it.

## Four signs you have

Not vague unease. Specific symptoms.

- **Enquiries get dropped.** Not lost exactly, just never followed up a second time. If you cannot say with confidence that every enquiry from last week got three attempts, the inbox is leaking.
- **Nobody can see the pipeline but the person holding it.** One person knows which enquiries are live. They go on holiday, or leave, and that knowledge goes with them.
- **You cannot state your conversion rate.** Not estimate. State. If "how many of last month's enquiries became jobs?" requires an afternoon of scrolling, you are managing without instruments.
- **The same question gets asked twice.** A buyer who already explained their situation gets asked again by someone else, because the first conversation lived in one person's head.

One of these is a process problem. Three or four is a tooling problem.

> Do not buy software to fix a process you have not written down. You will get the same chaos, with a subscription.

## The order to do this in

The expensive mistake is leading with the purchase. Do it the other way round.

**First, write down the process you actually want.** What happens in the first five minutes of an enquiry arriving. Who calls. How many follow-up attempts, over how many days, through which channels. What makes an enquiry dead rather than dormant. One page, no jargon.

**Second, run that process in the inbox for a month.** If it works there, you have a process. If it does not survive contact with a busy week, a CRM would not have saved it either. Fix it now, while it is cheap to change.

**Third, buy the smallest thing that automates the process you proved.** Not the most featured. The smallest.

## What "the right size" looks like

When you do move, the things that genuinely matter are unglamorous:

- **Enquiries arrive automatically**, from your forms and your phone, with the source attached. If anyone is copy-pasting enquiries into the CRM by hand, it will stop happening within a fortnight.
- **A shared view of what is live.** Everyone can see every open enquiry and who owns it.
- **Follow-up that prompts you.** The system tells someone that an enquiry from Tuesday has had no second attempt, rather than relying on memory.
- **Instant acknowledgement on arrival**, so the buyer knows they are handled while a human gets to them.
- **A conversion number you can read in one place**, broken down by source.

Notice what is absent from that list: forecasting, lead scoring models, dashboards nobody opens. Those become useful later, if at all. They are not why you are buying.

## The non-negotiable, whichever you use

One thing has to be true in either setup: the enquiry must arrive with its source attached.

An enquiry that lands as an anonymous form notification is a lost data point. You will convert it or not, and either way you learn nothing about which campaign, which page or which search produced it. Do that for six months and you have no evidence to optimise on, however good your follow-up is.

So whether the destination is Gmail or something grander, wire the source through: campaign, keyword, page, and whether it came by form or phone. Then record the outcome against it. That single habit is worth more than any feature comparison, because it is what lets you spend the next budget on the thing that worked.

## And on ownership

Whatever you end up with, it should be in your accounts, in your name, exportable.

The whole point of building a pipeline rather than renting one is that it remains yours. A CRM holding your enquiry history that you cannot export, or that an agency controls, reintroduces exactly the dependency you were trying to escape. Ask about export before you ask about features.
