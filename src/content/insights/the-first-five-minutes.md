Of all the ways to get more work from the same marketing budget, this is the one that costs nothing and gets ignored most.

Not better ads. Not a redesigned website. Just answering faster.

## Why speed beats quality

A buyer with a live need is in a particular state of mind. The deadline is real, the task is on today's list, and they want it dealt with. They have probably contacted two or three firms, or filled in one form that went to several.

The first firm to respond gets to frame the whole conversation. They set expectations, answer the actual questions, and start a relationship. By the time the second firm calls, the buyer is already comparing them against someone specific rather than against nothing. By the fourth call, they have usually booked.

Nobody in this position is thinking about who has the better accreditations. They are thinking about getting it sorted.

> Your competitors are not beating you on expertise. They are beating you by picking up the phone.

The uncomfortable corollary: if you are slow, you are paying to generate enquiries that someone else converts. Every pound of ad spend is effectively subsidising a faster rival.

## What "fast" actually means

Not "within one working day". Not "by end of play".

Minutes. The practical target for a live enquiry is a response in under fifteen minutes during working hours, and the firms that win consistently are often inside five.

That sounds impossible for a small team already on site all day, and it is, if "response" has to mean a full conversation. It does not.

## The two-layer answer

Split responding into two jobs and it becomes achievable.

**Layer one is automatic and immediate.** The moment an enquiry lands, they get an acknowledgement: you have it, here is who will be in touch, here is roughly when, here is a number if it is urgent. This can be an email, a text, a WhatsApp message. It takes no human time and it does something valuable: it stops the buyer carrying on down their list, because they feel handled.

**Layer two is human and considered.** A real person calls, with the enquiry details already in front of them, within the hour. Because the qualifying questions were asked on the form, that call can be about the job rather than about gathering basics.

Layer one buys you the time to do layer two properly. Most firms attempt only layer two, which means being either instant or absent, and in practice absent.

## The leak nobody measures

Ask a firm what proportion of inbound calls go unanswered and you will get a confident, wrong answer. It is almost always higher than they think.

Calls come in while everyone is on site. They ring out, go to a voicemail nobody checks, or hit an engaged tone. The enquiry simply evaporates, and because nothing was recorded, it never appears in any report. You paid for the click and never knew the call happened.

Call tracking makes this visible: how many calls, how many answered, how long until pickup, which campaigns produced them. The first month of that data is usually the most valuable thing in a new account, and the fix is rarely sophisticated. A diverted number, a shared responsibility for the phone between site visits, a call-back routine that actually gets followed.

## Then follow up more than once

One attempt is not follow-up. People are driving, on a roof, in a meeting.

Three or four attempts across the first couple of days, varying the channel — call, then text, then email — will recover a meaningful share of enquiries that a single missed call would have written off. Not pestering. Just acknowledging that busy people are hard to reach, which, given your buyers work in construction and property, they reliably are.

None of this requires a bigger budget. It requires deciding that an enquiry is worth five minutes of someone's attention on the day it arrives.
