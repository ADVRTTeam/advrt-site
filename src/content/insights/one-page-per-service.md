Here is the most common paid-search mistake in the built environment, and it costs firms more than any bidding decision: every campaign points at the homepage.

It is an understandable instinct. The homepage is the best page on the site. It has the portfolio, the accreditations, the full service list and the strongest design. And it is still the wrong destination, for a reason that has nothing to do with how good it is.

## A homepage is a compromise by definition

Your homepage has to serve a homeowner who wants a Level 2 survey, a solicitor chasing a probate valuation, a commercial landlord with an EPC deadline and a graduate looking at your careers page. Serving all of them means leading with none of them.

So the visitor who searched for one specific thing arrives and has to go looking for it. Most of the time they do not go looking. They go back.

Now picture the alternative. Someone searches "level 3 building survey Southampton" and lands on a page whose headline is Level 3 building surveys in Southampton. Everything below it is about that: what the survey covers, what it costs, how fast you can book, what the report looks like. There is nothing to hunt for and nothing to dismiss.

## Why Google rewards the same thing

Relevance is not only a conversion issue. It is a cost issue.

Google scores how well your ad, your keyword and your landing page line up. A tightly matched page lifts that score, and a better score means better ad positions at lower cost per click. You end up paying less for traffic that converts more, from the same change.

- **The headline matches the search**, so the visitor knows instantly they are in the right place.
- **The page covers one decision**, so the content answers the actual question rather than six adjacent ones.
- **The call to action is specific**, so there is one obvious next step instead of a menu.

> A landing page is not a smaller website. It is one argument, made properly.

## It makes your reporting honest

The quieter benefit is that focused pages tell you where the money is.

When every campaign lands on the homepage, your reporting collapses into one undifferentiated pile of enquiries. You cannot tell which services are profitable, which are a distraction, or where to put the next thousand pounds.

Give each service its own page and each one reports separately: cost per enquiry, conversion rate, cost per won job. Within a couple of months the pattern is obvious. Push budget towards the work with the best margins, pull it back from the rest, and stop guessing.

## You do not have to rebuild your website

This is the objection we hear most, and it is based on a false choice.

If you like your website, keep it. Focused landing pages can sit alongside it, on your own domain, matched to your branding, with the campaigns pointed at them instead of the homepage. Your site carries on doing its job for organic search and for people who already know your name.

If your website is the thing holding you back, that is a different conversation and the pages become the beginning of a proper lead-hub build. But it is a choice, not a prerequisite. The aim is to make your marketing work harder, not to replace what already works.

One more thing worth saying plainly: these pages have to be fast. Every second of load time costs conversions, and a slow page undoes the relevance you just paid for. Build them light.
