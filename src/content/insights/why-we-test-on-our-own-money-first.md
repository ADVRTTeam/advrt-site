Here is how agency onboarding usually works, stated plainly because it is rarely admitted.

A firm signs up. The agency has not worked in their sector before, or has worked in it shallowly. So the first two or three months are spent finding out which keywords convert, which ad copy lands, which questions a form needs to ask. Some of it works, much of it does not, and the whole education is paid for out of the client's ad budget.

The agency calls this the learning phase. From the other side of the table it is a tuition fee, and it is charged to the student who already knew their own industry.

We decided to pay it ourselves instead.

## The lab

Alongside client work, we build and run our own lead generation brands in the built environment. Our own domains, our own campaigns, our own money at risk. They are real businesses generating real enquiries, and they double as a permanent testing ground.

The most useful of them for surveying work is FindMySurveyor. It is not a demo. It runs continuously on our own spend and produces, currently:

Those numbers matter less as a boast than as a baseline. They mean that when we discuss what a surveying campaign should achieve, we are not extrapolating from a case study we read. We are reporting from an account we pay for.

> If a strategy has not worked on our own money, it has no business being tested on yours.

## What it actually tells us

The useful knowledge from running your own brands is more specific than "we know the sector".

- **Which search terms convert, and which merely attract clicks.** Plenty of high-volume keywords in this industry produce enquiries that never become instructions. Finding that out costs money. We have spent it.
- **Where the cheaper margins hide.** Lower-competition terms that the obvious bidding strategies miss entirely.
- **What a form can ask before completion rates fall.** The exact point at which one more question costs you more enquiries than it saves you phone calls. That is tuned, not guessed.
- **What the ad copy should say.** Which claims, in which order, in language the buyer actually uses.
- **How buyers behave after the enquiry.** Response windows, follow-up patterns, which enquiries go cold and how fast.

That last one is only learnable if you handle the leads yourself, which on our own brands we do.

## What this means for your account

The practical consequence is a shorter road.

When we build for a surveying firm, the campaign structure, keyword themes, match types and form questions all start from what is converting on our own surveyor campaigns right now. Day one is not a guessing game. It is an adaptation of something already working, tuned to your patch, your service list and the instructions you actually want.

The same applies across the sectors we run brands in: property, compliance, green home improvement and retrofit, trade-led home improvement. Different verticals, same principle.

To be clear about the limits, because overclaiming here would be exactly the behaviour we are describing: this does not make a new account instantly optimal. Every firm has its own fees, geography, capacity and competitive situation, and those take real data to tune. What it does is remove the expensive, generic part of the learning curve, so your budget is spent on refinement rather than on discovering fundamentals we already know.

## Why we are telling you this

Partly because it is the main reason to pick us over a generalist, and partly because it is a fair test to apply to anyone bidding for this work.

Ask an agency what they know about your sector and how they came to know it. Ask whether they have ever spent their own money in it. The answers are usually revealing, and the question costs you nothing to ask.
