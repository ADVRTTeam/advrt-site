Most search markets are discovered after they are already contested. Commercial EPC and MEES is the unusual case where the growth is written into legislation, the timetable is public, and almost nobody has built for it yet.

## Why this one is different

The Minimum Energy Efficiency Standard sets a floor on the EPC rating a commercial property must hold to be lawfully let. That floor has moved before and is set to move again, and every tightening converts a tranche of previously compliant buildings into non-compliant ones overnight.

For a landlord with a portfolio, that is a planning problem with a date on it. For an EPC assessor or building consultant, it is demand you can see coming.

That changes the calculus on content. Ranking a new page takes months. Normally that lag is a cost. Here it is the entire point: a page built now is mature and ranking by the time the searches arrive, and the firms that start when the searches arrive will be eighteen months behind.

## The searches that matter are not about certificates

Search "commercial EPC" and most of what comes back is transactional — assessors selling certificates on price and turnaround. That is a real market and a thin one. The certificate is a commodity, it is bought reluctantly, and it competes on being cheapest.

The queries worth having are the ones a landlord types when they are working out what to *do*:

- What the minimum EPC rating for commercial property is, and when it changes
- What happens if a commercial property falls below the standard
- Whether a property can still be let with a sub-standard rating
- What an EPC improvement strategy involves and what it costs
- Which exemptions exist, and how to register one
- How to improve a commercial EPC rating from one band to another

These are not certificate buyers. They are landlords with a portfolio problem, a compliance deadline and a budget to allocate. The certificate is a line item in a much larger conversation, and the firm that helps them think it through is in the room for all of it.

## What the buyer actually needs

A landlord facing MEES does not have a certificate problem. They have three questions, in order:

**Where am I now?** Which properties in the portfolio sit at which rating, which are already non-compliant, and which will be at the next tightening. That is an assessment exercise across a portfolio, not a single EPC.

**What do I have to do?** For each sub-standard property: what improvements would move it, what they cost, which are economically sensible against the rent, and where an exemption is genuinely available rather than wishful.

**In what order?** With a fixed budget and a fixed deadline, which buildings get done first. This is prioritisation work, and it is consulting, not inspection.

Almost no assessor's website speaks to any of this. They sell certificates. The gap between what the market sells and what the buyer needs is the opportunity, and it is a large one, because the second conversation is worth many multiples of the first.

## How to build for it

**A MEES explainer page as the anchor.** What the standard requires, the timetable as it currently stands, what non-compliance means in practice, and what the exemptions genuinely cover. Dated, maintained, and honest about what is confirmed versus proposed — this is a policy area that moves, and a page that is obviously current beats a page that is merely optimised.

**Improvement-route pages.** How a building moves from one band to another, what measures typically deliver what uplift, and realistic cost ranges. This is where you prove you understand buildings rather than forms, and it is the content landlords actually bookmark.

**A portfolio page.** Aimed squarely at the multi-property landlord, offering an assessment across the portfolio with a prioritised plan. Different page, different buyer, different value. This is the commercial destination the rest of the content should feed.

**Keep the certificate page.** It catches the transactional searches and it funds the work. Just stop letting it be the only page.

## The honest sizing

Commercial EPC is currently the smallest cluster of all the compliance disciplines we track. If you are looking for volume this quarter, this is not where to find it.

What it has instead is a legislated growth curve and a near-empty field. Building now is a bet on a timetable rather than on a trend, which is a considerably safer bet than most marketing involves.

MEES is simply the clearest case of it, because this time you can see the demand coming before it arrives.
